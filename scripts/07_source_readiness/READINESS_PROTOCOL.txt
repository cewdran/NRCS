4. REMAINING ANALYTICAL TRACKS
Adapt NIFA's questions to verified NRCS design rather than copying its thresholds.
- Diversity/community: justified additional alpha metrics, phylogenetic distances
  only with suitable trees, and repeated-plot models only where estimable.
- Taxa: frozen-protocol HPC rerun of legacy genus tests; prespecified broader
  differential-abundance models, filtering, contrasts and sensitivity checks.
- Functions: representative sequences plus matched unrarefied target counts;
  validated references/domain support; PICRUSt2 gene-family and pathway profiles;
  global composition and limited pathway contrasts with declared multiplicity.
- Soil/crop: eligible repeated measurements, soil gradients and comparable ionome
  panels; preserve source units, censoring, timing and field independence.
- Integration: a small declared predictor set, confirmed matches, treatment/time
  adjustment where estimable, and dependence-aware uncertainty.
All numerical preprocessing and scientific outputs run on Puma.

5. MODELING FEASIBILITY AND VALIDATION
After readiness review, freeze the exact outcome IDs, cohorts, predictor sets,
transformations, contrasts and model alternatives in an immutable protocol.
Choose outcomes by biological relevance and coverage, not by prior p-values.
Start with parsimonious repeated-measures models where identifiable. Plot
intercepts or slopes are not automatically estimable from sparse observations.
Use suitable compositional microbial responses; raw proportions are not
independent unconstrained response variables. Do not add thousands of predictors.
Finlay-Wilkinson is an environmental-response analogy, not an automatic temporal
forecast. State-space or MDSINE-type interaction models require stronger temporal
information; the latter additionally requires microbial-load information in its
original implementation. Neither is the default method for these field data.
For supported forecasts, train on earlier events and evaluate later events.
Compare against persistence and appropriate simple treatment/season baselines.
Separate predicting later observations on known plots from transfer to new plots
or farms. Block validation accordingly. Fit scaling, imputation, feature selection
and dimensionality reduction within each training set. Report prediction errors,
interval coverage and baseline-relative skill, with their sampling uncertainty.
Few held-out occasions may prevent reliable validation even when a model fits.
Report inability to validate instead of treating a training fit as predictive skill.

6. TEN-YEAR SCENARIO GATE
Define the origin as the last eligible observation for each modeled system;
state the calendar end date explicitly. Ten years is a scenario horizon, not an
already demonstrated forecast horizon. Near-term skill alone does not validate it.
Quantitative long-term simulation needs a suitable process model or relevant
external long-term calibration evidence, plus actual starting states and driver
records. Audit climate, irrigation, amendments, fertilizer, crop/management history,
soil texture and (for stocks) depth and bulk density as appropriate to the model.
Candidate scenarios: continued management under a defined climate distribution;
continued management with greater water limitation; a specified management change.
Do not assume future management or invent missing rates. Use documented assumptions
and distinguish probabilistic uncertainty within a scenario from differences
between scenarios. Propagate initial-state, parameter, driver and model uncertainty;
show sensitivity to literature-derived assumptions and transferability limits.
If support is insufficient, use literature-grounded conceptual trajectories and
testable hypotheses only. Wider intervals do not validate unsupported extrapolation.
Allow improvement, little change and deterioration; short-term null results do not
establish delayed benefit. No ten-year effect sizes are generated in this package.

7. FOUR-MANUSCRIPT STRUCTURE
Each farm manuscript will include a final Discussion subsection:
"Potential long-term trajectories and testable microbial functions".
Oatman: nutrient turnover/organic matter and eligible grain yield/composition links.
Page Springs: vineyard management and potential nutrient availability pathways.
Whipstone: within-system annual/blackberry nutrient-cycling responses and limits.
Cross-farm: comparable within-farm contrasts, shared/divergent potential functions,
context-dependent scenario responses and uncertainty. Three farms do not provide
independent replication of crop, location and management effects. Keep unique
questions, disclose shared samples, and avoid duplicate principal results.
For every proposed functional statement, retain an evidence row with observed
response, inferred genetic potential, plausible process, projected direction/range
(if supported), assumptions, uncertainty, alternatives and validation measurements.
16S-inferred pathways are not measured genes, expression, enzymes, nutrient flux or
plant benefit. Taxon names alone do not establish functions. External studies
support hypotheses; they do not establish that these functions occurred here.
