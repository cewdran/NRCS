4. Scalar functional responses
Reuse the saved per-sample relative abundance normalized within its complete
original EC or MetaCyc profile. For each of the fourteen planned functions,
response = late minus early relative abundance for each matched plot. An
absent feature identifier (in_original_table=False) is missing for inference,
not an observed structural-zero feature. Hold that planned test as untestable.
Report matched early/late means, mean changes, ranges and independent counts
by treatment. A label-permutation omnibus tests whether mean changes differ
among the declared treatment groups. It is not a test of the pooled temporal
change already reported, nor proof that a particular treatment differs from
another. No pairwise treatment-change claim follows from the omnibus alone.

5. Full functional profile responses
Normalize within each complete EC, KO or MetaCyc matrix, then take square
roots (Hellinger coordinates). For each matched plot, form the full coordinate
vector late minus early. Validate complete-profile sums, sample identities,
eligibility and reproduction of the saved targeted relative abundances on Puma.
Do not use a selected-feature normalization or an ordination-axis shortcut.
The omnibus response is the complete Hellinger change vector. Its null concerns
the distribution of those vectors across treatment labels; the statistic
measures separation of group mean vectors in both direction and magnitude.
Report the norm of each group's mean change vector separately from its mean
individual Hellinger and Bray–Curtis turnover distances. Those quantities answer
different questions and must never be substituted for one another. Retain
group dispersion context and avoid a pure location interpretation if spread
or imbalance could contribute to the omnibus association.

6. Soil repeated-plot panels
Use corrected soil observations, not microbiome eligibility. Retain the valid
June 2024 Oatman sheep-East soil-only record. Exact fixed panels are declared
in CONFIG, with nine Oatman plots across November 2023, June 2024 and December
2024, and eight Grenache plots across October 2023 and August 2024. Additional
baseline plots and the two November 2024 Grenache observations remain
descriptive and must not be relabelled as August observations. A missing,
duplicate, censored, nonfinite, unit-mismatched or treatment-inconsistent fixed
panel record makes its planned full-panel test untestable; do not silently
substitute a smaller panel. Preserve all observations and reasons in audits.

Two separate estimands per endpoint:
  A. Average level: each plot's mean across its fixed dates, in reported units.
     Compare these independent plot averages across treatment groups. This
     evaluates separation over the observed visits and can include pre-existing
     baseline differences. Balanced fixed dates give each visit equal weight.
  B. Temporal change: a vector of each later visit minus the fixed baseline
     value for each plot. Compare the complete change vectors across groups.
     For Grenache this is one late-minus-early coordinate. This is a categorical
     treatment-by-date pattern question, not a fitted linear annual trend.
Baseline here means the first observed study visit. It does not imply a
confirmed measurement before treatment began.
Never shuffle visits within a plot or pool soil visits as independent samples.
A non-rejection of B neither proves equal trajectories nor establishes that
an earlier treatment difference persisted. Interpret A and B separately.

8. Omnibus statistic and label allocations
Each input has one independent row per physical plot. Center response columns,
form the plot-by-plot Gram matrix, and compute the between-group centroid sum
of squares. Statistic R2 = between-group SS / total centered SS. At fixed
response, group sizes and dimension this orders allocations identically to
the corresponding between/within pseudo-F. P-values come only from label
allocations, not from a parametric F distribution. Constant or invalid
responses are explicit untestable rows. SS roundoff guards are scale-aware.

Permute treatment labels across whole plot responses, preserving every arm's
count and every plot's paired measurements or temporal vector. No unverified
spatial restriction is invented. Exact enumeration includes every distinct
multiset allocation when the space is at most 5000. Otherwise use 19999 seeded
with-replacement allocations with p = (1 + at-least-observed count) / 20000.
Exact p is its at-least-observed count divided by all allocations. Include
ties with the documented scale-aware equality tolerance. Report the number
of allocations, actual tail count, mode and seed. Label symmetries can make
the attainable minimum p coarser than the nominal allocation probability grid.
Canonical plot order and cohort/arm inventory bind the seed schedule.
Functional endpoints with the same cohort use a common schedule derived from
base seed 20261016. Soil seeds hash the frozen endpoint, estimand and design.
Changing a depth cohort changes its schedule and is recorded, rather than
being treated as the same population.

Inference is conditional on whole-plot label exchangeability under the null.
Permutation does not establish randomization, independence, identical dispersion
or causal treatment effects. Small, imbalanced field groups warrant cautious
association language. More permutations cannot create more independent plots.
